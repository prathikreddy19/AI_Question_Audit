# Product Decisions

## 1. Who loses the most when a question is broken, and how does the design reflect that?

Students are the most affected when a question contains errors because it can directly impact their learning and exam preparation. Faculty members are also affected since reviewing every report manually is time-consuming.

The system prioritizes students by making it simple to report issues directly from the question interface. Reports are then analyzed by AI to reduce faculty workload, but every recommendation still requires faculty approval before any change is made. This keeps students protected while maintaining academic quality.

---

## 2. What would you never let the AI auto-apply, and why?

I would never allow the AI to automatically modify or publish question content.

Academic questions influence student learning and assessments. Even a highly accurate AI can misunderstand subject context or introduce subtle mistakes. Every AI recommendation should therefore be reviewed by a faculty member before it becomes part of the question bank.

The AI acts as an assistant, not the final decision maker.

---

## 3. One metric you would watch during the first week after launch

**Average Report Resolution Time**

This measures the time from when a student submits a report until a faculty member approves or rejects it.

A decreasing resolution time indicates that:
- AI recommendations are useful.
- Faculty workflow is efficient.
- Students receive faster corrections.

This metric directly reflects the effectiveness of the overall review process.

---

## 4. One idea beyond this brief that I would pitch to Acadza

Introduce a **Question Quality Score** for every question.

The score would be calculated using factors such as:
- Number of student reports
- Faculty corrections
- AI confidence
- Historical accuracy
- Time since last review

Questions with lower quality scores could automatically be prioritized for review. This would help maintain a high-quality question bank proactively instead of waiting for multiple student reports.
